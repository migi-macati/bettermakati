# BetterMakati journalism and editorial standards

Applies to **all existing and future Featured Reports, Insights and articles**, in English and Filipino/Taglish. This is an internal editorial policy, not citizen-facing filler.

## Publication gates

1. **Public-interest test.** State the specific Makati question, affected residents/institutions, what is new or consequential, and why an article deserves its own page. Do not publish low-value rewrites of announcements. Consolidate duplicates; preserve canonical records and redirects.
2. **Verification.** Check each material factual claim against the best available original record (ordinance, resolution, signed document, agency release, budget table, procurement file, court ruling, original interview or dataset). Verify dates, names, boundaries, units, fiscal years, status, denominators and source vintage. A press release is evidence of what an institution *said*, not independent proof that promised outcomes occurred.
3. **Corroboration and attribution.** Seek independent corroboration for consequential or disputed assertions. Attribute single-source claims explicitly. Identify the publisher, publication/event date, direct source URL, retrieval/check date and relevant page/table where possible. Do not invent citations, quotes, interviewees, attendance, outcomes or inaccessible source contents.
4. **Facts, analysis and uncertainty.** Clearly distinguish observed facts, institutional claims, calculations, inference, scenarios and unknowns. Show reproducible methods, formulas, comparison periods, nominal/real distinctions, assumptions, exclusions and limitations. Never imply causation from correlation.
5. **Fairness and right of reply.** Represent material competing explanations proportionately to evidence, without false balance. For serious adverse claims about identifiable people or organizations, seek and document a meaningful opportunity to respond before publication when feasible; otherwise clearly state that response was not obtained. Avoid guilt by association and political advocacy.
6. **Independence and conflicts.** Do not publish promotional copy disguised as reporting. Disclose relevant sponsorship, affiliations, financial interests, assistance or editorial conflicts. Advertising and political interests cannot dictate factual conclusions.
7. **Harm, privacy and law.** Minimize identifying details about private individuals, minors, victims and complainants. Handle allegations, court matters and sensitive health/safety information carefully; do not imply a finding of wrongdoing without sufficient evidence. Avoid personal data in public URLs or analytics.
8. **Writing and accessibility.** Use precise, economical, natural English and modern Taglish. Specific, informative headlines; lead with the answer and strongest evidence. No clickbait, unsupported superlatives, artificial certainty, sermons, repetitive caveats or inflated completeness claims. Make tables readable, dates unambiguous, sources accessible and translations substantively equivalent.
9. **Editorial review.** Before merging, check every claim/source pair, jurisdiction, dates, figures, opposing evidence, headline accuracy, image rights/credits, accessibility, broken links, related-page propagation, build and required CI. High-impact investigations require a second substantive review where available; never claim a review that did not happen.
10. **Corrections and transparency.** Correct material errors promptly across all affected pages. Record what changed, when and why, distinguishing correction, clarification and update. Do not silently erase substantive errors or rewrite history; retain internal provenance and the original publication date.
11. **Editorial disposition.** Rate each existing piece: **retain**, **update**, **merge**, **re-report**, or **withdraw**. Assess factual integrity first, then public value, depth, originality, timeliness and duplication. Withdrawal requires documented reason and preservation of archival/source references as appropriate.
12. **Release rule.** If a central claim lacks adequate evidence, hold the piece or narrow the claim. Passing a build is not fact-checking; editorial signoff is not proof of deployment.

## Required article review record

- Stable packet ID, slug, owner, review date, original publication date and proposed disposition.
- Public-interest question and one-sentence evidence-backed finding.
- Claim ledger: claim; type (fact / attributed claim / analysis / scenario / unknown); exact source and location; checked date; corroboration; confidence; action.
- Data and calculation audit, including year/vintage, currency, inflation and denominators where applicable.
- Material contrary evidence, responses sought/received, conflicts and privacy/legal considerations.
- English/Taglish parity; headline and summary audit; related pages to update.
- Editorial verdict, unresolved blockers, CI status, merge SHA, production SHA and rendered-page verification.

## Workflow for existing content

Start with consequential or heavily surfaced articles, contested claims, numerical analyses and dated claims. Review in bounded packets; avoid whole-site rewrites. Publish sourced corrections before stylistic improvements. Do not delete an article merely for being old if it retains historical value. Where evidence is insufficient, label the limitation and re-report or withdraw as warranted. Maintain separate Civic and Featured Reports queues and stable IDs.
