# W7-9 — Wave 7 closure

Status: closed-verified  
Reviewed: 2026-10-01

## Closure decision

Wave 7 is complete. The site now has a coherent Makati visual identity, differentiated but related page families, purposeful photography, BetterBarangay parity, editorial treatment for evidence-rich material, distinct civic-asset treatments and stronger responsive/accessibility evidence.

This closure is a stopping rule as much as a status record. It avoids turning visual polish into an indefinite redesign cycle.

## Benchmark synthesis

The closure review used three comparison rings.

### BetterLGU ecosystem

BetterLGU portals establish useful shared expectations around service discovery, city information, officials/contact information and civic navigation. BetterMakati should remain recognizably part of that ecosystem without becoming a lightly customized template.

### Public digital services

Current patterns reviewed across GOV.UK, NYC, Amsterdam, LifeSG and Transport for London reinforce task clarity, accessibility, contextual information and reusable patterns. A useful synthesis for BetterMakati is: consistent, not uniform. Services, budgets, reports, history, barangays and routes do not need identical layouts when their user jobs differ.

### High-end web design

Contemporary visual-design references reinforce a distinction between aesthetic impact and functional visual design. BetterMakati therefore does not need every page to behave like a showcase. Stronger art direction belongs where it improves discovery, reading, place identity or understanding; transactional and data-dense surfaces should prioritize comprehension.

These comparisons are design inputs, not an objective claim that BetterMakati is the world's best or most beautiful civic site.

## Keep

- Hero → Around Makati → Common Services as the homepage opening sequence.
- Search and task entry points ahead of decorative spectacle.
- Purposeful Makati photography with accessible carousel behavior and shared framing.
- Deep green as the civic foundation with restrained gold accents.
- Different compositions for services, evidence/accountability, editorial pages, barangays and civic assets within one design language.
- Existing cross-page relationships that let services, reports, places, records, history and barangays hand users to relevant context.
- W7-8 responsive and accessibility coverage, including five representative viewport sizes and the expanded 200% text-reflow journey.
- Editorial hierarchy already established for Reports, History and Heritage.
- Distinct treatments for places, bounded infrastructure and network-service routes.

## Reject

- A universal page template that erases information-type differences.
- Another structural homepage redesign without evidence of a user problem.
- A giant full-screen photo hero or moving search below imagery.
- Decorative hero/photo overlaps, gradients or animation added only for visual novelty.
- More slogans, completeness claims, explanatory banners or other sermon-like copy.
- Another Reports/History/Heritage overhaul.
- A new photography-acquisition phase when the existing library already supports the current design.
- Reopening Wave 7 merely to pursue an unverifiable “best-looking” claim.

## Accessibility and responsive evidence

W7-8 expanded representative browser coverage across the major page families. The 200% text-resize check exposed genuine layout defects in Projects & Budget and Statistics. Those components were changed to retain stacked layouts longer under enlarged text rather than hiding overflow or weakening the assertion.

The final W7-8d branch run #2251 passed. After merge, independent `main` run #2252 also passed.

## Result

Wave 7 leaves BetterMakati with a deliberate visual system rather than a single repeated template: civic utility remains primary, Makati identity is visible, editorial surfaces can carry stronger art direction, and responsive/accessibility protections remain part of the definition of visual quality.

The next planned product phase is Wave 8: a whole-site English/Filipino language experience using natural modern Taglish where Filipino is selected, without conyo phrasing or unnecessary respelling of familiar English loanwords.
